How to Get a High Score in Dhamaka Blocks

This guide goes deeper than the basics. If you already understand the rules and want to know exactly how to push your score from 200 to 500+, this is the breakdown you need. We will cover the full scoring formula, how to engineer combo streaks, and walk through a sample high-score run turn by turn.

Understanding the Scoring System

High scores in Dhamaka Blocks come from three stacking mechanisms. Most players only use the first one.

Scoring Mechanism How Much When It Activates
Base points1 pt per cell placedEvery placement
1-line clear bonus+10 ptsComplete any row or column
2-line clear bonus+35 ptsTwo lines cleared with one piece
3-line clear bonus+75 ptsThree lines cleared with one piece
4-line clear bonus+130 ptsFour lines cleared with one piece
5+ line clear bonus+200 ptsFive or more lines cleared with one piece
Full board clear+500 pts flatThe entire 8×8 grid is empty after a clear

On top of the bonus table, a combo multiplier (covered next) scales the line-clear bonus based on how many turns in a row you've cleared at least one line. The key insight: a single well-timed placement that clears 4 lines on your 4th consecutive clearing turn scores base cells + (130 bonus × 5.0) — 650 bonus points from one move alone. That is the difference between a 200-point game and a 1,000-point game.

The Combo Multiplier — Your Biggest Score Driver

The combo multiplier is what separates casual players from high scorers. To keep a combo alive, you need to clear at least one line every single turn — not every few turns. This requires building parallel lines simultaneously. The multiplier climbs across six tiers the longer your streak runs:

Consecutive Clearing Turns Multiplier Voice Cue
1 turn1.0×—
2 turns2.0×AMAZING
3 turns3.5×ON FIRE
4 turns5.0×UNSTOPPABLE
5 turns7.0×LEGENDARY
6+ turns10.0×UNREAL

How to Set Up a Combo Chain

The core technique is to work on two separate lines at the same time rather than completing one fully before starting the next. Here is how it looks in practice:

Sample 6-turn combo chain
Turn 1 Place pieces to fill row 7 to 7/8 and start filling column 2 No clear yet
Turn 2 Complete row 7 — 1 line clear. Continue column 2 to 6/8 +10 bonus, ×1.0
Turn 3 Complete column 2 — 1 line clear. Start row 3 in parallel +10 × 2.0 = 20 pts
Turn 4 Complete row 3 while also finishing column 6 — 2-line clear +35 × 3.5 = 122 pts
Turn 5 3×3 square placed, clears row 5 and column 4 — 2-line clear +35 × 5.0 = 175 pts
Turn 6 One more single-line clear to extend the streak to 6 turns +10 × 10.0 = 100 pts

Notice how sharply the multiplier compounds from turn 4 onward. Every turn you extend the streak is worth dramatically more than the last — reaching the 6-turn "UNREAL" tier turns even a modest single-line clear into a 100-point swing. Let the streak break once and you reset to 1.0× — rebuilding from scratch costs you the compounding, so protecting an active streak is worth more than chasing one big piece.

The Corner-First Strategy

The corner-first approach is not just about grid management — it is a scoring strategy. When you anchor your placements in a corner and build outward in a wave, you create the conditions for multi-line clears. Here is why:

As you fill a corner region, rows and columns in that area approach completion simultaneously. A single piece placed in the right spot can complete both the row and column it lands in at once. That is a guaranteed 2-line clear bonus (+35) instead of two separate 1-line clears (+10 each). Two lines at once beats two lines separately by 15 points before the combo multiplier — and once your streak is running at 3.5× or higher, that 15-point gap becomes 50+ points per placement.

Recommended Opening Sequence

  1. First 3 pieces: Fill the bottom-left 3×3 zone of the grid as completely as possible using the first tray.
  2. Next 3 pieces: Extend rightward along the bottom two rows. Aim to get rows 7 and 8 both to 6/8 full before clearing either.
  3. Next tray: Complete row 8 and row 7 in the same or consecutive turns to start the combo.
  4. From turn 5+: Maintain the combo by always having a line within 1–2 cells of completion before the current tray runs out.

Setting Up Multi-Line Clears

A multi-line clear — clearing two or more lines with a single piece placement — is worth disproportionately more than sequential single-line clears. The 3-line bonus (+75) versus three separate 1-line clears (+30 total) is already two and a half times the points from that one moment, and every point of that gap gets multiplied again by your combo tier. At a 5.0× streak, the difference is 375 bonus points versus 150.

The Intersection Technique

The most reliable way to engineer multi-line clears is to leave the intersection cell of a nearly-complete row and column unfilled. When both the row and column are at 7/8, the one remaining cell they share is the intersection. Placing any piece that covers that cell clears both simultaneously.

For example: row 4 is full except column 3, and column 3 is full except row 4. The cell at (row 4, column 3) is the intersection. A 1×1 single block placed there clears both lines at once for a 2-line clear bonus (+35) plus 1 base point.

The L-Shape Double Clear

L-shaped pieces are the workhorses of multi-line clears. An L that has its corner at the intersection of two nearly-complete lines can fill the last cell of both the row and column simultaneously — while also contributing cells to a third line you are building in parallel. This is the most efficient move type in the game for score density.

The 3×3 Square and Boosters — Your Advanced Score Levers

The 3×3 square unlocks the moment you cross 200 points into Level 2 — not at the top of the level ladder — so it's available for the vast majority of every run. It places 9 cells at once, generating 9 base points in a single move, and because it spans three rows and three columns at once, a well-placed 3×3 is the single most likely piece to trigger a multi-line clear.

A 3×3 placed to clear 3 lines on your 4th consecutive clearing turn: 9 base + (75 bonus × 5.0) = 384 points from one move. That single move is worth more than most players' entire first 100 points.

Boosters Change the Math

v2.6.0 added three coin-funded boosters that give you direct control over the variables above, rather than just hoping the right piece appears:

  • REFRESH (40 coins) — if your current tray has no piece that sets up a multi-line clear, refreshing it can hand you exactly the shape you need instead of wasting a turn on a suboptimal placement.
  • UNDO (30 coins) — reverses your last placement. Use it the instant you realise a placement broke a combo streak you were building, before the multiplier resets.
  • BOMB (60 coins) — clears a 3×3 area of filled cells around a tapped cell. It doesn't score points itself, but it can carve out the open 3×3 zone you need to safely land the piece above, or rescue a fragmented grid that would otherwise end your run before you reach a big combo.

None of these cost you a turn's placement, so spending coins on the right booster at the right moment is often more efficient than playing around a bad tray or a broken combo.

How to Score Faster Overall

  • Prioritise multi-line clears over single-line clears — the bonus points and the compounding combo multiplier accelerate your score far faster than base points alone.
  • Keep the combo active from Level 1 onward — the jump from 1.0× to 2.0× at just two consecutive clears is the single biggest early-game swing in the game.
  • Save coins for a REFRESH or BOMB when you're mid-combo and the tray or grid threatens to break your streak — protecting an active streak is usually worth more than the coins themselves.

Sample High Score Run — Turn by Turn

Here is a condensed walkthrough of a 500+ point game strategy. This assumes you have read the sections above and understand the combo and multi-line concepts.

1
Turns 1–3: Build the Corner (Score: ~18)

Use the first tray to pack the bottom-left corner tightly. Place an L-piece into the corner, a 1×3 horizontal along the bottom, and a 2×2 square in the adjacent space. No clears yet — this is setup. Score: roughly 18 base points.

2
Turns 4–6: First Clears, Start Combo (Score: ~55)

Extend along rows 7 and 8. Use the second tray to get both rows to 7/8 full. On turn 6, place the piece that completes row 8. That is your first line clear (+10 bonus at 1.0×). Combo streak begins. Score jumps to ~55 with the bonus.

3
Turns 7–9: Sustain the Streak to 2.0× (Score: ~130)

Complete row 7 on turn 7 — second consecutive clear, multiplier jumps to 2.0×. Start working on column 1 in parallel. Complete it on turn 9 for another 2.0× bonus. Score: ~130, closing in on Level 2 (200 points).

4
Turns 10–13: Reach 3.5× and Level 2 (Score: ~260)

A third consecutive clear locks in 3.5×. Target the intersection technique: set up row 5 and column 5 to both be at 7/8, then clear both simultaneously with one piece for a 2-line bonus (+35) at 3.5× = 122 bonus points in one move. Score: ~260 — Level 2 reached, the 3×3 square is now in the piece pool.

5
Turns 14–20: Compound the Multiplier (Score: ~550)

Keep the combo alive toward the 5.0× and 7.0× tiers. If the tray stops giving you setup pieces, spend 40 coins on REFRESH rather than break the streak. Each 2-line clear now adds 35 bonus points multiplied 5–7×, worth 175–245 points per placement. By turn 20 you should be past Level 3 (500 points) with the streak still running.

6
Turn 21+: The 3×3 Endgame Play (Score: 1,000+)

You have kept a 3×3 open zone free in the top-right corner since Level 2. When the 3×3 piece appears in your tray, place it there, clearing 3 lines simultaneously (75 bonus × 7.0 = 525 bonus, plus 9 base cells × 7.0 = 63). That one move generates roughly 588 points. If the grid gets fragmented before you can land it, a 60-coin BOMB can clear the landing zone without costing you a turn. With continued multi-line clears, 1,000+ total is within reach in the next 3–5 turns.
