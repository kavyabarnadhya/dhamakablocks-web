Why "Made in India" Mobile Games Are Having a Moment

More players are actively looking for mobile games built by Indian studios, not just games with an Indian theme bolted on. That shift favors homegrown developers who understand the local audience — familiar card games, festival aesthetics, and cricket, cooked into apps by teams who grew up with them rather than teams guessing from outside.

It also means the definition matters more than ever. A game can look Indian on the surface — orange-and-gold color schemes, a diya icon — without an Indian team anywhere near its codebase. Knowing the difference is the point of this guide.

What Actually Counts as a Made-in-India Game?

A made-in-India game is one built by a studio founded and headquartered in India — not just a game that uses Indian characters, colors, or festival themes without an Indian team behind it. The studio's home base, not the app's marketing art, is what decides the label.

By that standard, a lot of games with an "Indian" veneer don't actually qualify, while some of the country's biggest mobile hits — often board games, card games, and cricket titles — clearly do. Below are a few well-known examples, each with a real Indian studio behind it.
