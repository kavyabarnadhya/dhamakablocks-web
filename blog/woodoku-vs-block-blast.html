What's the Difference Between Woodoku and Block Blast?

The difference most players notice first is board size and theme, not the underlying rules. Both games hand you a tray of pieces, ask you to drag them onto a grid, and clear the grid by filling complete lines — that basic loop is shared across the entire block-puzzle genre, including Dhamaka Blocks. Woodoku is commonly associated with a wood-carved aesthetic and a 9×9 board divided into 3×3 boxes, borrowing visual language from Sudoku. Block Blast tends to run on a more classic 8×8 layout with a simpler, brighter palette.

Beyond the visuals, many players report that the two feel similar to actually play once you're a few minutes in — the puzzle-solving tension of "will this last piece fit anywhere" is the core appeal in both. Where they tend to diverge is presentation: one leans into a calm, wooden aesthetic, the other into a punchier, saturated one.

Which Has the Bigger Grid?

Woodoku is the one typically built around a larger board. A 9×9 grid with 3×3 sub-boxes gives you more surface area to plan around before a game ends, which some players find more forgiving in the early game. An 8×8 grid, used by Block Blast and by Dhamaka Blocks, fills up faster and tends to reward tighter, more deliberate placement from the first move.

Is There a Block Puzzle Alternative With a Different Look and No Extra Complexity?

Yes — Dhamaka Blocks is a third option worth trying if you like the Woodoku-or-Block-Blast style of play but want a fresh visual identity. It's built by Dhamaka Games, an Indian studio, around an 8×8 grid with the same tray-of-3 mechanic, wrapped in a saffron, gold and peacock-green festive theme with rangoli-inspired visuals and Indian percussion sound effects. There's no timer and no lives — the game ends only when none of your current 3 pieces fit anywhere on the grid.

Where it stands apart on mechanics is the combo system: clearing lines on consecutive turns builds a multiplier from 1.0× up to 10.0× at six or more consecutive clears, and missing a clear resets the streak back to 1.0×.
